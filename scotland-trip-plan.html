At a glance

A clockwise loop with six Scottish bases: trains for the cities (Oct 17–22), then one rental car from Inverness for the Highlands (Oct 24–Nov 1). Every town on Luke's list is covered.

  • Travelers: Bill (79) and Luke (49). Luke does all the driving.
  • Dates: Land at London Heathrow around 7 am on Oct 16, then train to York for the night. Scotland Oct 17–31 (14 nights). Fly Glasgow → Heathrow Oct 31, sleep near Heathrow, fly home from Heathrow Nov 1.
  • Budget: about $100 (roughly £75) a night per room, staying in towns rather than Edinburgh or Glasgow.
  • Pace rule: no more than one long travel day in a row, at least two nights per base where possible, and one unscheduled rest day each week.
Time pressure: the trip starts in 11 days. Lodging, the rental car, travel insurance and the UK entry permit (ETA) need booking this week.

Getting around

Use one rental car, not three: pick it up in Inverness on Oct 24 and drop it at Glasgow Airport on Oct 31. Trains cover Edinburgh, Glasgow and Dumbarton easily from Stirling, and the Inverness car carries them to Oban.

Trains (Oct 16–23)

  • Every train leg is direct except the Edinburgh change on Oct 17.
  • Book free Passenger Assist for Bill: staff help with bags, boarding and seating. Book at least 2 hours before travel.
  • Advance tickets on the Scottish routes start around £11–£15 one way. A Two Together Railcard (two named adults travelling together) cuts fares by about a third.

Car (Oct 24 – Nov 1)

  • Luke should be the only driver. Many UK rental companies set an upper age limit, often 75 or 79, or ask older drivers for a doctor's note.
  • Book an automatic. UK rentals default to manual (stick shift).
  • Pick a small car. Skye has single-track roads with passing places.
  • The main driver needs a credit card in his own name. A one-way drop fee will apply.

Bill's health and safety

Most important: travel medical insurance for both of them, including medical evacuation. Medicare generally does not pay for care outside the US.

Before leaving

  • Pack all medications in carry-on, in original containers, plus an extra week's supply.
  • On the flight: walk the aisle every hour or two and drink water.

On the trip

  • Emergency number is 999 (112 also works). For urgent but not emergency advice, call NHS 111.
  • Note the nearest hospital at each base when checking in.
  • Cold wind and hills add strain. Dress in layers and take taxis up steep streets.
  • Ask every hotel for a ground-floor room or a lift (elevator). Many small B&Bs have only stairs.

Steep spots to see from below or skip

  • Dumbarton Castle: hundreds of steep steps up the rock.
  • Wallace Monument near Stirling: a narrow spiral staircase to the top.
  • Dunnottar Castle: steep steps down to the castle and back up; the clifftop view is the best part anyway.
  • Edinburgh and Stirling castles: steep, cobbled approaches. Taxi to the gate and ask about mobility help.
  • McCaig's Tower in Oban: steep climb from town; drive up instead.

Before-departure checklist

The first two items can stop the trip if missed, so do them this week. (Tick boxes reset when the page reloads.)

  • UK ETA for both Bill and Luke. £20 each, only at gov.uk/eta or the official UK ETA app. Airlines will refuse boarding without it. Avoid lookalike sites that charge more.
  • Travel medical insurance for both travelers, including medical evacuation.
  • Book the Oct 31 Glasgow → Heathrow flight (early afternoon, checked bag included). Book a Heathrow pickup car and a flexible King's Cross → York train ticket.
  • Book 8 lodgings with free cancellation: York, Stirling, Stonehaven, Inverness, Skye, Fort William, Oban, and a Heathrow airport hotel. Ask for twin beds and ground floor or a lift.
  • Book the rental car: automatic, small, Luke as driver, Inverness pickup Oct 24, drop at Glasgow Airport Oct 31.
  • Buy a Two Together Railcard and book train tickets in advance.
  • Book Passenger Assist for each train leg.
  • Set up phone service abroad and download offline maps (signal is patchy on Skye).
  • Tell banks about the trip; bring a credit card with no foreign transaction fees.
  • Share this plan and lodging addresses with family at home.
